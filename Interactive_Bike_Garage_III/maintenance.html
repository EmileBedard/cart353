Maintenance Hot Tips

Replace a punctured tube

Replacing a punctured tube is one of the most common repair any cyclist encounters.

1. Start by uninstalling your wheel from your frame using the bolt or quick release lever.
2. Empty any remaining air in your inner tube before removing your tire using tire levers.
3. Remove or patch the punctured and replace with a sealed innertube that respects your tire and rim dimensions.
4. Reinstall your wheel and be very careful to not pinch your new tube. Preferably, don't use tire levers for this step.

Voila! Happy riding.

Reroute your chain

Rerouting your chain is common and you need to dive right in and don't be scared of a little grease on your hands.
(Pro tip!: Use dishsoap as a great degresear to clean chain grease from your hands)

1. Start by pulling in the last Jockey wheel of your derailleur to remove tension from the chain.
2. Analyze and locate where the chain is stuck. Get in there and pull!
3. Replace chain on the right cassette cog while letting go of your derailleur.
4. Spin a few pedal strokes to re-align chain on the right speed that you were set on.

Voila! Happy riding.

Check your chain

Check your chain every few months depending on your riding frequency to make sure your chain is still usable. Chains are consumable parts and you want to replce it as soon as it is no longer in great shape to prevent damage on your plateau and cassette wich are much more expensive to replace!

Use a chain checker to make sure the chain is in good shape. Your checker tool should not fit in the next chain hole. If so: REPLACE!

Voila! Happy riding.

Lube your chain

Chains are mechanical systems that feature metal on metal dynamics. On a bike, everytime two metals meet: YOU SHOULD HAVE GREASE!
If you bike casually or don't want to worry about performance --- use standard wet lube made for bike (NEVER use WD-40)
If you want to consider more speed and less maintenance, consider waxing your chain! New was drips are becoming more common but you can always use a hot crockpot with wax to coat your chain --- check online for full process, more common for road or gravel bikes.

Clean your bike

A clean bike is a fast bike !

1. foam and soap it all ( be careful to not degrease your pivot points or bearings.)
2. Spray it all! (same thing here, dont spray your bearings)
3. Dry it all! (This is the most important step! Cleaning is great but metal+water=rust, be careful)

Adjust your derailleur

When pedaling, you should not encounter major click sounds or unexpected shifting. If so, you may consider re-alignment of your rear or front derailleur.

Derailleur alignment and indexing can be hassle, be patient. You will need to adjust your H, L and B limit screws to reach optimal alignment.

1. Start by shifting to your smallest cog. There adjust "H" limit screw to match derailleur position with the cog placement
2. Adjust your indexing with your barrel adjuster so that evry speed change makes the derailleur go up or donw one speed
3. Shift to your highest gear and adjust the derailleur positionment with the "L" limit screw.
4. Finish with adjusting your "B" limit screw so the spacing between the first jockey wheel and smallest cog is enough and nothing rubs.

Uninstall and reinstall your pedals

Uninstalling and reinstalling can be tricky because both sides don't have the same threads and thread direction

The Pro tip is the remember the universal rules for pedal threading. rotating using an 8mm hex key on the other side of the cranks or a pedal wrench, you need to turn towards the front of the no matter the side to tighten and toward the rear to loosen

FRONT ----> TIGHTEN
BACK ---> LOOSEN
